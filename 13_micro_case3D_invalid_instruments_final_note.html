1 Introduction

This micro case illustrates the risks associated with using multiple instruments when some of them may be invalid. While adding instruments can increase precision, invalid instruments can reintroduce bias into IV estimators.

2 Data Generating Process

We simulate a setting with one valid instrument and one invalid instrument. The invalid instrument directly affects the outcome, violating the exclusion restriction.

6 Overidentification Test

Note.
The Sargan test is based on a chi-square distribution and therefore does not have a denominator degrees of freedom (unlike F-tests), which is why df2 is reported as NA.

The Sargan test evaluates whether the instruments are jointly valid. Rejection suggests at least one invalid instrument, but non-rejection does not guarantee validity.

7 Interpretation

Adding invalid instruments can bias IV estimates, even when the model is overidentified. Overidentification tests are useful diagnostics, but they cannot confirm instrument validity.

8 Conclusion

This case demonstrates that more instruments are not necessarily better. Instrument validity is more important than instrument quantity.
